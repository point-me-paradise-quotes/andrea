Read the island overview and our suggestions

Oahu: History, Variety & Easy Logistics

Best for: Pearl Harbor, a mix of activities, and the easiest flights.

  • Pearl Harbor is here, and it's a powerful visit for history lovers. It would make a great guys' morning while the ladies enjoy a spa day.
  • The North Shore is laid-back, with big beaches, surf towns, shrimp trucks, and sea turtles. It feels nothing like Waikiki.
  • Activities: fishing charters, snorkeling, ziplining, scenic hikes, and dolphin encounters.
  • Where we'd suggest staying: Turtle Bay on the North Shore is one of Christina's favorite places in Hawaii. It's spacious, set on its own stretch of coastline, and has a lovely spa. It's a quieter base, with Honolulu and Pearl Harbor about an hour away.

Maui: Familiar Ground, a New Corner

Best for: long beach days, easy resort living, and a group that wants to settle in.

  • You know Maui already, so this would be about a different side of it rather than a first look.
  • South Maui's Wailea is walkable: from the villas we've quoted, Wailea Beach and The Shops at Wailea are a minute or two on foot, so dinners out don't need a car every night.
  • West Maui's Ka‘anapali coast is the other choice, with snorkeling straight off the beach at Kahekili.
  • Activities: snorkeling, pool and beach days, and golf, with three Wailea courses five minutes from Wailea Beach Villas.
  • Where we'd suggest staying: the Ho‘olei Villas give you full use of Grand Wailea's pools, waterslides and restaurants, while the Wailea Beach Villas put a private home with resort service a minute from the sand.

Kauai: Lush, Quiet & Breathtaking

Best for: scenery, adventure, and a slower pace.

  • Dramatic cliffs and rainforest, with the Nā Pali Coast and Waimea Canyon among the most beautiful places in the islands.
  • Excellent fishing: Christina works with a wonderful fishing guide here who would be a great fit for your catch-and-cook day.
  • Activities: ziplining, hiking, snorkeling, kayaking, and helicopter tours.
  • Where we'd suggest staying: 1 Hotel Hanalei Bay is one of Christina's most beloved places to stay anywhere. It sits on the North Shore overlooking Hanalei Bay, with the Bamford Spa and a calm, nature-centered feel.

A Day on the Big Island for the Volcanoes

Hawaii Volcanoes National Park is on the Big Island, and it's the only place to see Hawaii's active volcanoes. The island is very large, with long drives between areas, so rather than a base we'd suggest it as a day trip from whichever island you choose.

  • Hilo is the closest airport to the park, about 45 minutes away; from Kona it's two hours or more.
  • We'd fly you over on a full-size jet and can arrange a private guide for the day.
  • Volcanic activity changes constantly, so flowing lava is never certain. Even without it, the craters, steam vents, and lava fields are remarkable.
  • The day trip isn't included in this quote; we'll price it once you've settled on your island.

Getting Between Islands (Full-Size Planes Only)

We know small planes are a hard no for Brittany. We'll book inter-island flights only on full-size jets with Hawaiian Airlines or Southwest, never small commuter planes. Most flights between islands are under an hour. (Inter-island flights are not included in this quote.)

Our Suggested Approach

Option 1: Oahu base + Big Island day trip. Pearl Harbor, the North Shore, and Turtle Bay or a private home, with one day flying over to see the volcanoes.

Option 2: Oahu + Kauai. Split the stay between the two islands, with Pearl Harbor and variety on Oahu, then fishing, scenery, and quiet on Kauai. The Big Island day trip can be added if the volcanoes are a priority.

Option 3: Maui, done differently. If returning to familiar ground appeals, the Wailea villas make an easy, walkable base, and the Big Island day trip can be added from there too.
