The sailing

Out of Galveston on a Sunday afternoon, back the next Sunday morning

Symphony of the Seas pulls away from Galveston at 3 p.m. on January 31 and spends its first two days crossing the Gulf. That's the honest shape of this itinerary: three full sea days to two-and-a-half port days, which makes the ship itself a bigger part of the week than on a port-heavy route. Choose your stateroom with that in mind.

The port run comes in the middle — Costa Maya, Roatán, then Cozumel on three straight days — before one last sea day and an early 7 a.m. arrival back in Galveston.

Below are six stateroom options for the two of you, from an inside cabin to an ocean-facing room with a larger balcony. Pick the one you're leaning toward and the estimate updates as you go.

Your stateroom options

Six ways to spend the week aboard

All six are priced for two guests sharing one stateroom. The real differences come down to light, outdoor space, and what you look at from it.

Inside or window

The interior has no window — dark and quiet for sleeping. The ocean view adds a window and daylight, but no balcony.

Balconies facing in

The Boardwalk and Central Park balconies look inward over two of the ship's open-air neighborhoods, not out to sea. You get your own outdoor space, with the ship's life going on below.

Balconies facing the sea

The ocean view balcony and the large-balcony stateroom face outward. Category 1C is the same idea with more room outside to stretch out.
