Why Kauaʻi

The oldest island, and it shows

Kauaʻi is the oldest of the main Hawaiian Islands — roughly five million years of rain and surf working on volcanic rock. That time is what you see: fluted green cliffs on the Nā Pali Coast, a canyon cut more than 3,000 feet deep, rivers you can paddle, and reef-sheltered beaches between them. It's the most rural-feeling of the main islands. Towns are small, the speed limit rarely tops 50, and roosters wander parking lots everywhere — the kids will notice within ten minutes of landing.

One thing that surprises people: there is no road around the island. The highway runs about three-quarters of the way and stops at each end of the Nā Pali cliffs. So where you stay decides which half of the island is on your doorstep, and which half is a day trip.

Options 1 & 2North Shore — Princeville

The greener, quieter, more dramatic side. Hanalei Bay, the taro fields of Hanalei Valley, Kīlauea Lighthouse and the start of the Nā Pali trail are all close.

The honest trade for February: winter is the North Shore's wet, big-surf season. Rain showers come and go, and Hanalei Bay can have waves that make swimming unsafe on some days. Waimea Canyon is a long drive — plan on two and a half hours each way.

Options 3 & 4South Shore — Poʻipū

Drier and sunnier, with calmer water in winter — the easier bet for daily beach time with kids. Waimea Canyon is about an hour's drive, and the airport under half an hour.

The honest trade: it's more built-up and busier than Princeville, and the landscape is drier and less lush. The Nā Pali trailhead and Hanalei become a full-day trip.

Experiences worth building the week around

Nature, beaches and sightseeing

Not included  None of these are in the quote price. Tell us which ones appeal and we'll book them — and your island-wide destination guides will cover all of these and more.

See the Nā Pali Coast

Seventeen miles of sea cliffs with no road. A catamaran or raft from the west side, or a helicopter flight, shows you what most visitors never reach. In winter, ocean conditions decide what the boats can do on a given day.

Best from: either shore

Waimea Canyon & Kalalau Lookout

Red and green canyon walls, then the road climbs to Kalalau Lookout, where you look straight down into a Nā Pali valley — if the clouds cooperate, so go early. Pack a jacket; it's cooler up there.

Best from: South Shore

Humpback whale season

February sits in the middle of whale season. You can often see spouts and breaches from shore at Poʻipū and from Kīlauea Point, or get closer on a whale-watch cruise.

Best from: either shore

Hanalei & the end of the road

Stop at Hanalei Valley Lookout over the taro fields, walk the pier on Hanalei Bay, get shave ice, then drive out to Keʻe Beach. The first two miles of the Kalalau Trail start there — steep and muddy in winter. Hāʻena State Park has required advance reservations; we'll check and book.

Best from: North Shore

Kīlauea Point Lighthouse

A red-capped 1913 lighthouse on a seabird refuge. Winter is nesting season for Laysan albatross, and you can watch frigatebirds and boobies ride the wind below the cliff.

Best from: North Shore

Poʻipū beaches & Spouting Horn

Green sea turtles feed close to shore, and Hawaiian monk seals haul out on the sand to sleep (stay well back). Five minutes away, Spouting Horn blasts seawater up through a lava tube.

Best from: South Shore

Mahaʻulepu Heritage Trail

An easy coastal walk over wind-carved limestone and sea cliffs, starting by Shipwreck Beach. Go at sunrise and you may have it nearly to yourselves.

Best from: South Shore

Paddle the Wailua River

A guided kayak up Hawaiʻi's only navigable river, then a hike through the jungle to a waterfall. A full morning, best for kids who are comfortable paddling and walking a couple of muddy miles.

Best from: either shore

Gardens, and what to eat

Allerton Garden near Poʻipū has the giant buttressed fig roots kids want to climb (guided tours only). For food: a bowl of saimin at Hamura Saimin in Līhuʻe, poke from Kauai Poke Co., and dinner at Eating House 1849 in Poʻipū.

Best from: South Shore
