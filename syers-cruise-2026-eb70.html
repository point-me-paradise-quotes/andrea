Sailing A · Celebrity Cruises

Celebrity Summit — 7-Night Alaska Dawes Glacier Cruise

June 20–27, 2027 · Round-trip Vancouver · Veranda/Balcony stateroom

The earliest of the three, and the only one in June — long northern daylight, snow still high on the mountains above the fjords. The glacier day is Endicott Arm, a narrow fjord that ends at the face of Dawes Glacier, with the ship in the arm from 1:00 to 6:00 p.m.

  • June 20 — Depart Vancouver at 4:30 p.m.
  • June 21 — Scenic cruising through the Inside Passage
  • June 22 — Endicott Arm and Dawes Glacier, 1:00–6:00 p.m.
  • June 23 — Skagway, 7:00 a.m.–8:00 p.m.
  • June 24 — Juneau, 7:00 a.m.–6:00 p.m.
  • June 25 — Ketchikan, 1:30–8:00 p.m.
  • June 26 — Scenic cruising through the Inside Passage
  • June 27 — Arrive in Vancouver at 7:00 a.m.

The honest trade: lowest price of the three on either fare, and the June date. No Glacier Bay — the glacier day is Dawes Glacier instead.

August 28 – September 4, 2027 · Round-trip Vancouver · Stateroom type: your advisor will confirm

  • Sep 2 — Ketchikan, 11:00 a.m.–7:00 p.m.
  • Sep 3 — Scenic cruising through the Inside Passage
  • Sep 4 — Arrive in Vancouver at 7:00 a.m.

The honest trade: Glacier Bay plus Tracy Arm, round-trip from Vancouver so flights stay simple — at the highest cruise-only price of the three.

The latest sailing, into mid-September, when the crowds in port thin out. Glacier Bay gets nine hours on September 16, and the last full day is spent at sea on the way down to Seattle rather than back through the Inside Passage.

Round-trip (closed-loop) cruise

A closed-loop cruise starts and ends at the same port — you board and step off in the same city. Sailings A and B both do this from Vancouver.

Why people like it: one round-trip flight in and out of the same airport, which is usually simpler to arrange, and you know the pier and the city by the time you return.

One-way cruise

A one-way cruise starts in one port and finishes in another. Sailing C boards in Vancouver and ends in Seattle.

Why people like it: you don't retrace the same stretch of water, and you get two different cities at either end of the trip. The trade is flights into one city and home from another, which can change the airfare.

All three sailings here begin in Canada. Andrea will go over the travel documents you'll need — the official U.S. State Department (travel.state.gov) and Government of Canada travel pages are the places to check.
