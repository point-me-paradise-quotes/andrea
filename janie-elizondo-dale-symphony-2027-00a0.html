The ship and the ports

Symphony of the Seas

One of Royal Caribbean's Oasis-class ships, and among the largest cruise ships sailing. The ship is split into neighborhoods, so a group of friends can spread out by day — Central Park's planted walkways, the Boardwalk with its carousel, the pool deck — and still find each other for dinner.

Three sea days mean the ship is part of the trip, not just transport. That's the honest trade with a ship this size: you get plenty to do onboard, and you share it with a lot of other guests.

Why a balcony for this trip

With three sea days and early port arrivals, a balcony gives you a quiet place for coffee before the day starts — somewhere that's yours, away from the busy decks. For an anniversary week, that's often the part people remember.

Other room types can be priced once you've chosen a sailing.

Costa Maya, Mexico

A cruise port on Mexico's southern Caribbean coast, next to the fishing village of Mahahual. Expect a beach strip of palapa restaurants, ceviche, and cold drinks a short walk from the pier, with Maya ruins inland for anyone wanting a longer day.

Roatán, Honduras

The largest of Honduras' Bay Islands, sitting on the Mesoamerican Barrier Reef. Snorkeling and diving are the draw here, along with West Bay's long white beach. An early start — the ship is in from 7:00 AM.

Cozumel, Mexico

An island off the Yucatán coast with clear water and reef snorkeling close to shore. The longest port day of the week, until 6:00 PM — time for a beach club, or tacos al pastor in San Miguel before heading back aboard.

Once you're booked, you'll get a port guide for each stop. Shore excursions are not included in this quote. We can recommend and price them once you've chosen a sailing.
