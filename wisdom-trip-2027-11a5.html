Ocho Rios sits on Jamaica's north coast, about an hour and a half to two hours by resort transfer from Montego Bay. The Great House Luxury room sits close to the property's restaurants and bars — mahogany furniture, a private balcony, a shower/tub combo bathroom. It's an adults-only, all-in stay: meals, top-shelf pours, pools, land and water activities, entertainment, Wi-Fi, gratuities, and round-trip transfers are already folded into the total below.

Beach: one long stretch of white sand split into three areas — a lively main beach, a calmer lagoon, and a dedicated watersports beach — with clear, generally calm water. Pools: seven pools and four whirlpools, including a lively main pool with a swim-up bar and the quieter Great House Pool tucked back near the butler village. Dining: 16 restaurants covering Caribbean, French, Italian, Japanese, and Southern American menus — Kimonos for hibachi-style teppanyaki, Soy Sushi Bar, Kelly's Dockside for over-the-water seafood, Le Gourmand for French, Valentino's for Italian, the Jerk Shack for jerk chicken, and Southern Table for breakfast. Bars: swim-up bars at most pools, beachfront spots like the Oceans Beach Bar, and The Rabbit Hole speakeasy for evening cocktails and live entertainment.

Azulik isn't a beach resort in the usual sense — it's built up into the Tulum jungle on elevated wooden walkways, with rooms designed to look out rather than in. The Jungle Villa has a queen bed, jungle views, an indoor bathtub, an outdoor Mayan-style tub, air conditioning, a ceiling fan, and Wi-Fi. Not included meals, alcoholic beverages, spa treatments, and other extras run separately from the room rate — Azulik's model is closer to a boutique hotel than a traditional all-inclusive.

Beach: a private stretch of shoreline with a beach club for daytime lounging and sunset dining — there's no communal swimming pool here; instead, private plunge pools and outdoor tubs are built into select villas. Dining: three restaurants — Kin Toh, a tasting-menu restaurant built into treetop "nests" 12 meters up with Mayan-avant-garde plates; Tseen Ja, Japanese with sea views; and Cenote, the most casual of the three, open from breakfast through dinner with a farm-to-table, vegetarian-friendly menu. Bars: several spread across the property, including a mezcalería, with a drinks program that leans botanical and herb-forward rather than classic tiki.

SLS Playa Mujeres reads more like a design hotel than a classic all-inclusive — beachfront setting, multiple pools, upscale dining, and the brand's usual social energy, with meals, drinks, and activities included in the rate. It isn't an adults-only property, but it's built with distinct adult-facing spaces and a more grown-up feel than a typical family all-inclusive nearby. It's also the most convenient of the four for a short trip — about 35 to 45 minutes from Cancún International Airport.

Beach: a private beach with white sand and calm, clear water, free loungers and umbrellas, and three beach bars. Pools: four pools, including Privilege, an adults-only pool with a swim-up bar and DJ-driven music. Dining: 18 restaurants and bars — Fi'lia for handmade pasta and wood-fired pizza, Union for New York-style steakhouse fare, Caracol for wood-fired seafood from chef Hugo Ortega, and Smoke & Mirrors, a speakeasy-style cigar and cocktail bar.

Zemi Miches sits on the Dominican Republic's northeastern coast, in El Seibo province — a newer, quieter stretch of coastline than the main Punta Cana hotel zone, with mountains behind it and open beach in front. The resort combines Taíno- and Dominican-inspired design with multiple restaurants, bars, and pools as part of the all-inclusive rate. It's the newest of the four and the quietest setting — the trade-off is the longest airport transfer of the group.

Beach: Playa Esmeralda, a white-sand beach along the resort's private stretch of coastline. Pools: four pools, including a rooftop infinity pool for Club Azure guests and private plunge pools built into ground-floor suites. Dining: around 17 restaurants, bars, and lounges spanning Caribbean, Taíno, Thai, and Amalfi Coast menus — Anacaona serves local Taíno-inspired cuisine, with additional venues for Dominican coffee and casual all-day fare. Bars: a lobby bar, a beachside cocktail bar, and multiple swim-up pool bars.
